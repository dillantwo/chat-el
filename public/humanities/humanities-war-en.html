Hong Kong in the War of Resistance against Japanese Aggression

From 1941 to 1945, Hong Kong lived through a dark period known as the "three years and eight months". This is more than a memory of war. It is an important part of our history, showing the strong will of the people of Hong Kong and their spirit of defending their home and country.

⏳ Key Moments in Hong Kong's History

1
The September 18th Incident

Japanese troops blew up a section of railway at Liutiaohu near Shenyang, falsely blamed Chinese troops for it, and then occupied the three provinces of Northeast China. This set off a nationwide movement to resist Japan and save the nation, and marked the start of the 14-year War of Resistance.

2

The Fall of Hong Kong: Dark Years and the Suffering of the People

Japanese Atrocities in Hong Kong

On 25 December 1941, the Japanese army formally took over Hong Kong. Historians call this day "Black Christmas".

1. Hardship under Japanese Rule

After the occupation, the Japanese army used extremely harsh measures against the people of Hong Kong to tighten its control.

The Japanese army imposed martial law in Hong Kong. Soldiers searched homes and arrested and killed civilians at will. To ease the shortage of food, the Japanese authorities also carried out a policy of reducing the population, forcing residents to return to their home villages. Large numbers of people left this "hell on earth" for the Mainland. The Japanese army sent people to the South China coast on junks, with no regard for their safety. Many junks were robbed on the way. Some were towed out to the open sea and then sunk or set on fire by the Japanese, and afterwards many burnt bodies drifted ashore. Along the roads home, refugees passed old people who had collapsed and died and babies who had been abandoned.

At the same time, the area around Lockhart Road in Wan Chai became a centre of "comfort stations", where women were forced to serve Japanese soldiers. Dr Li Shu-fan, who ran a hospital in Kowloon at the time, estimated that he treated at least 10,000 victims of rape during the Japanese Occupation (Ta Kung Wen Wei, 2025).

According to Contemporary China (2021), Hong Kong's population fell sharply from about 1.6 million before the war to only about 600,000 when Japan surrendered in 1945. This loss of a million people was mainly caused by deaths in the war, famine, and the forced repatriation carried out by the Japanese army to reduce the demand for food.

(Mini-drama The War of Resistance, Episode 2: Sacrifice – in Chinese)

Figure 1.1: The sharp fall in Hong Kong's population, 1941–1945 (Source: Contemporary China)

Japanese atrocities against civilians:

  • Brutal methods: The Japanese army often tortured people suspected of resistance, for example by forcing water into them or beating them, which filled society with fear (The University of Hong Kong, 2025).
  • Silver Mine Bay Massacre: Even after Japan announced its surrender in August 1945, some remaining Japanese troops at Silver Mine Bay on Lantau Island hunted down and arrested villagers without reason, killing and injuring innocent people.

After the war, Noma Kennosuke, commander of the Kempeitai (Japanese military police) in Hong Kong, was convicted of war crimes for his atrocities and was executed at Stanley Prison. This was a symbol that justice had been done (Ta Kung Wen Wei, 2025).

2. Economic Plunder: The Disaster of the Military Yen

To strip the people of Hong Kong of their wealth, the Japanese occupation government introduced the "military yen":

  • Forced to replace the Hong Kong dollar: From 1943, the Japanese authorities allowed only the military yen to be used. Anyone found holding Hong Kong dollars faced heavy punishment.
  • Savings turned into waste paper: After Japan surrendered, the Japanese side refused to exchange the military yen. Countless families in Hong Kong lost all their savings overnight, which was a heavy blow to people's lives after the war.

3. Shortage of Supplies: Memories of Hunger

The Japanese army supplied its own troops first, so ordinary people lived in great hardship:

  • The "6.4 taels" ration: Each person received a very small daily food ration, only about 6.4 taels (around 240 grams) of rice. Many people fell ill from malnutrition or hunger, and some even starved to death in the streets (Ta Kung Pao, 2025).
  • Seizing metal: To make war supplies, the Japanese army removed the bronze lions of the HSBC building and many bronze statues from the Victorian era.

4. Cultural Destruction: Forced "Japanization" of Education

To wipe out people's Chinese cultural identity, the Japanese army forced "Japanized" policies on Hong Kong:

  • Forced to learn Japanese: Schools had to teach Japanese, and even place names were changed to Japanese names. For example, Queen's Road Central was renamed "Nakameiji-dori".
  • Education abandoned: Most schools closed because of the war, so a whole generation of children lost the chance of a normal education.

⚔️ Brave Resistance: The Spirit of Defending Home and Country

Under the blockade of the Japanese Occupation, a group of heroes fought the invaders bravely for years, in both the countryside and the city of Hong Kong.

# Flames of Resistance: The Heroes of the Hong Kong-Kowloon Brigade behind Enemy Lines

1. The War of Resistance in South China and the Birth of the Dongjiang Column

In 1938, the Japanese army invaded Guangdong. This moved local people to form guerrilla units, made up mainly of farmers, students, seamen and other ordinary people. They were a self-defence force with wide support from the people. A guerrilla unit is an irregular military organisation. It moves quickly and flexibly and makes good use of the land to fight the enemy. The guerrillas' main tasks included gathering intelligence, carrying out rescues, and destroying the Japanese army's supply facilities to weaken its fighting strength. The guerrillas showed great patriotism and a strong sense of national unity. Even in harsh conditions, they kept up their determination to fight, showing how the Chinese nation stays strong and unyielding in hard times.

In October 1938, the Japanese army landed at Daya Bay in Huiyang, Guangdong. The Kuomintang defending troops quickly collapsed, and Guangzhou and large areas of the lower Dongjiang (East River) region fell. At that time, the Communist Party of China (CPC) set up a number of guerrilla units in Huiyang, Dongguan, Bao'an and other places, including the Huiyang-Bao'an People's Anti-Japanese Guerrilla Corps and the Dongguan-Bao'an-Huiyang Border People's Anti-Japanese Guerrilla Brigade. Between April and May 1939, these units accepted designations from the National Revolutionary Army and were reorganised as two brigades under the Dongjiang Guerrilla Command of the Fourth War Zone of the National Revolutionary Army. In September 1940, the two brigades were reorganised as the Guangdong People's Anti-Japanese Guerrillas, and in January 1942 as the Guangdong People's Anti-Japanese Guerrilla Corps. Following instructions from the CPC Central Committee, the CPC Guangdong Provincial Committee set up guerrilla zones behind the Japanese-occupied areas of the Dongjiang region and organised the people's armed forces against Japan. These forces gradually grew into what later became the Dongjiang Column.

In December 1943, the Dongjiang Column, formed by combining a number of people's armed forces, was officially founded. Its full name was the Dongjiang Column of the Guangdong People's Anti-Japanese Guerrilla. It was an anti-Japanese guerrilla force led by the Communist Party of China during the War of Resistance against Japanese Aggression.

"In the darkness, the Hong Kong-Kowloon Independent Brigade of the Dongjiang Column fought the enemy bravely to defend home and country."

Image source: Ta Kung Pao (2025)

This force fought a guerrilla war along the coast and in the mountains of Guangdong. It cut the Japanese army's transport routes, protected local people, and supported the nationwide counter-offensive against Japan. (Mini-drama The War of Resistance, Episode 5: Raid – in Chinese)

2. The Hong Kong-Kowloon Independent Brigade: Anti-Japanese Heroes among Us

In February 1942, the Hong Kong-Kowloon Brigade was formed at Wong Mo Ying Village in Sai Kung to defend Hong Kong in particular. What made this force special was that most of its members were young local patriots and ordinary people of Hong Kong. (Mini-drama The War of Resistance, Episode 4: Rescue – in Chinese)

Their brave deeds included:

  • Raids in the city: They sent pistol squads to ambush Japanese posts in the urban areas.
  • Attacks at sea: Using fishing boats as cover, they attacked Japanese ships carrying supplies.
  • Psychological warfare: They handed out anti-Japanese leaflets in the streets, telling people "We will win", which raised everyone's confidence.

3. The Great Rescue of Cultural Figures

The Hong Kong-Kowloon Brigade carried out an admirable mission. Under close searches by the Japanese army, it secretly rescued several hundred well-known cultural figures and patriots from Hong Kong, protecting the "seeds" of the nation's culture. (Mini-drama The War of Resistance, Episode 1: Rescue Operation – in Chinese)

4. Selfless Service after Victory

After Japan surrendered in 1945, the Hong Kong-Kowloon Brigade did not leave at once. Seeing what society needed at the time, its members chose to help keep law and order in the New Territories and protect villages from bandits until normal order returned. People in Hong Kong still remember this sense of duty to defend home and country today.

Times were very hard, but the spirit of the Hong Kong-Kowloon Brigade shows us that anyone can become a hero who protects their home, as long as they care for their home and country and are brave enough to take responsibility.
