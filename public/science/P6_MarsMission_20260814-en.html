Science fact card: Why is flying to Mars so hard?

  • Overcoming gravity: A rocket pushes gas downwards (the action force) and gets an upward push back (the reaction force). Only when the reaction force is greater than the pull of gravity can the rocket break free and reach space.
  • Stage separation high in the sky: As the rocket climbs away from the Earth's surface, each stage must be dropped once its fuel is used up, so that the spacecraft can keep speeding up into space.
  • Solar power: Once it is cruising in space, the spacecraft must open its solar panels to keep it powered during a long journey of several months.
  • Slowing down before reaching Mars: As it nears Mars, the spacecraft fires its retro-thrusters to brake and slow down before it can enter the Martian atmosphere.
  • Opening the parachute at the right time: If the parachute opens too high, the load on it is too great and it tears. If it opens too low, there is not enough distance left to slow down properly.
